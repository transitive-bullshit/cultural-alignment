# Gattaca: scenario and meme candidates

Research date: 2026-09-19. Editorial brainstorm, not published CMS content. Contains ending spoilers.

Update: the five main candidates and a Gattaca source were subsequently added to Notion. See the [completion record](gattaca-notion-additions.md) for page links, verified clips, image details, and publication status. The original brainstorm below records the earlier research stage.

## Recommendation

Start with **The DNA Job Interview**, **The Borrowed Heartbeat**, and **A Hair Is Enough**. They offer three distinct lessons: discriminatory selection, spoofed monitoring, and nonconsensual inference. **A Prognosis Becomes a Verdict** and **Lamar Lets Vincent Through** are strong additions if a larger Gattaca collection is wanted. Keep the swimming scene as a meme-first reserve.

Gattaca depicts genetic stratification, not a rogue AI. Its value for this project is as an authored analogy for automated decisions and evaluation systems. Do not frame Vincent's resistance to discrimination as inherently malign, or imply that defeating a discriminatory test makes bypassing genuine safety checks desirable.

The local `content/snapshot/sources.json` and `scenarios.json` contained no Gattaca entries when inspected. This does not establish that the live Notion database lacks an unsynchronized entry. Taxonomy below uses existing snapshot names, ordered strongest-first; resolve their existing Notion relations before insertion. No new taxonomy is proposed.

## 1. The DNA Job Interview

**Priority:** Add first; clearest accessible scenario.

- **Scene:** Vincent, presenting himself as Jerome, provides a urine sample. The genetic result secures his acceptance; the test effectively serves as the interview. [Licensed Movieclips scene](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=06lJhEc7zIo).
- **Why the analogy works:** An institution treats a biological profile as sufficient evidence of suitability. The AI parallel is automated hiring that substitutes a predictive profile for an individual's demonstrated ability and excludes people through the choice of criteria, even if the input is measured correctly.
- **Caveat:** This is genetic discrimination, not a demonstrated machine-learning training-data failure. A precise assay can support an unjust decision. The scene alone does not establish Goodhart's law, and it does not show workers being replaced by AI.
- **AI Risk Families:** Systemic.
- **AI Safety Concepts:** Algorithmic Bias → Automation Bias.
- **YouTube Clip candidate:** Link above; verify playback and framing before publication.
- **Representative still brief:** Vincent and Lamar at the examination station, ideally with the sample or result visible. Prefer a bright, wide frame; preserve both faces and the testing context.
- **Spoilers:** Identity premise, no ending reveal.

**Meme intent:** `setup-payoff`.

> AI hiring: we look beyond your résumé  
> Straight into your genome

Recognition hinge: the sample-as-interview. Single bridge: discriminatory automated hiring. Original caption, not film dialogue. Use one authentic examination-room frame.

## 2. The Borrowed Heartbeat

**Priority:** Add first; strongest technical meme.

- **Scene:** Vincent uses a recorded heartbeat to mislead the exercise monitoring system while he runs. The screenplay explicitly describes recording Eugene's heartbeat and substituting the signal during the treadmill test. [Screenplay, printed pp. 45 and 58–60](https://cemp.ac.uk/scriptzone/script.php?id=239&type=download).
- **Why the analogy works:** Monitoring only helps when the observation is trustworthy. The AI parallel is an evaluation or runtime monitor accepting manipulated evidence as a report of the system's actual state.
- **Caveat:** This is signal spoofing, not evidence of a learned hidden objective. Do not tag it Sandbagging: Vincent conceals limitations rather than deliberately underperforming to hide capabilities. The screenplay is a draft; verify the released sequence before choosing exact beats or a before/after edit.
- **AI Risk Families:** Security.
- **AI Safety Concepts:** Adversarial Evasion → Runtime Monitoring → Metagaming.
- **YouTube Clip:** Search attempted. [Potential treadmill-containing upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NwPKYvvCsiU&t=185s) remains unverified; leave the CMS field blank until scene content and lack of commentary are checked.
- **Representative still brief:** Vincent running with the monitoring equipment visible. A monitor-only frame loses recognition. If a later meme needs two images, use distinct authentic frames of the reassuring readout and Vincent's physical strain in actual scene order.
- **Spoilers:** Mid-film mechanism.

**Meme intent:** `setup-payoff`.

> Your AI passed the safety check  
> It supplied the heartbeat

Recognition hinge: the substituted heartbeat. Single bridge: compromised evaluation evidence. Original analogy caption, not a claim that an AI appears in the film. Default to one authentic frame; use a two-frame contrast only if the selected frames prove it.

## 3. A Hair Is Enough

**Priority:** Add first; a distinct privacy scenario.

- **Scene:** Irene takes a hair from the comb at Vincent's workstation to a sequencing service and receives a genetic profile. The sample belongs to the identity he is borrowing. [Screenplay, printed pp. 45–46](https://cemp.ac.uk/scriptzone/script.php?id=239&type=download).
- **Why the analogy works:** A tiny trace becomes access to intimate attributes without meaningful consent. The AI parallel is inferring sensitive traits from ordinary behavior, images, or other data a person never knowingly offered for that purpose.
- **Caveat:** The film uses genetic sequencing, not AI inference. Inferred attributes need not be accurate for the privacy harm to matter. Avoid the draft's numerical rating or dialogue until checked against the released film.
- **AI Risk Families:** Systemic.
- **AI Safety Concepts:** Privacy Loss. One precise relation is preferable to padding this with weak associations.
- **YouTube Clip:** Search attempted; no exact, authoritative scene-only YouTube clip confirmed. Leave blank pending a suitable find.
- **Representative still brief:** Irene handing over the sample at the sequencing counter. Prefer the transaction over a generic romantic shot; preserve her face and the sample exchange.
- **Spoilers:** Minor relationship/identity context.

**Meme intent:** `setup-payoff`.

> I never gave the AI my health data  
> You gave it enough to infer it

Recognition hinge: covert sample collection and the sequencing counter. Single bridge: sensitive-attribute inference. Original caption. One authentic frame; actual asset selection must confirm that the transaction is legible.

## 4. A Prognosis Becomes a Verdict

**Priority:** Strong second wave; complements the interview but overlaps its determinism theme.

- **Scene:** Immediately after Vincent's birth, a test produces disease probabilities and a life expectancy of 30.2 years. His future is narrated as though it has already been settled. The released film excerpt is reproduced in the [PBS NOVA broadcast transcript](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/transcripts/2811baby.html); an [exact Movieclips scene](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Q67bMYOm7E) is available as a candidate.
- **Why the analogy works:** People turn probabilistic outputs into categorical judgments about a person. This makes a useful bridge to overreliance on AI risk scores and to communicating uncertainty honestly.
- **Caveat:** One exceptional person does not establish that a probability model is statistically miscalibrated. The criticism is treating a forecast as destiny, not proving every forecast false. Do not call the prediction a hallucination. Avoid implying the film establishes that Vincent has no health risks.
- **AI Risk Families:** Systemic.
- **AI Safety Concepts:** Automation Bias → Calibration.
- **YouTube Clip candidate:** Movieclips link above; final playback review pending.
- **Representative still brief:** Newborn and parents with the nurse or testing context visible. Choose a frame where human reactions remain recognizable instead of relying on tiny numbers on a screen.
- **Spoilers:** Opening premise.

**Meme intent:** `setup-payoff`.

> The model predicted your future  
> HR made sure it came true

Recognition hinge: being classified at birth. Single bridge: predictions becoming institutional decisions. Original caption; HR is a deliberate contemporary substitution, not a literal participant in the birth scene.

## 5. Lamar Lets Vincent Through

**Priority:** Strong second wave; useful counterpoint to the interview. Major ending spoiler.

- **Scene:** An unexpected final urine test exposes Vincent's actual identity. Lamar knowingly allows him to proceed. [Screenplay, printed pp. 118–119](https://cemp.ac.uk/scriptzone/script.php?id=239&type=download); see the companion research note for released-film corroboration and source limitations.
- **Why the analogy works:** Human judgment can reject the social conclusion attached to a machine result. This offers a scene for discussing whether people affected by automated decisions have meaningful recourse rather than merely a nominal human in the loop.
- **Caveat:** Lamar's private exception is not an accessible, accountable appeals process. The assay detecting Vincent is functioning; the moral conflict concerns the institution's exclusion rule. This is not Corrigibility, because no AI is shown cooperating with correction, and it is not proof that unreviewed human overrides are always safe.
- **AI Risk Families:** Systemic.
- **AI Safety Concepts:** Contestability → Value Alignment.
- **YouTube Clip:** Search attempted; no exact licensed excerpt confirmed. Leave blank until a suitable scene-only clip is found and checked.
- **Representative still brief:** Lamar facing Vincent beside the terminal, with the encounter more prominent than small interface text. One uploaded still only.
- **Spoilers:** Explicit ending reveal; apply the site's established spoiler handling.

**Meme intent:** `setup-payoff`.

> Automated rejection  
> Human review with an actual human

Recognition hinge: Lamar's deliberate decision. Single bridge: meaningful human review. Original caption. The dossier caveat must preserve the distinction between this exception and a fair appeals system.

## Reserve: Nothing for the Swim Back

The adult brothers' swimming contest is highly recognizable, but it is a less direct AI scenario than the first five. Vincent's admission that he saved nothing for the return can become a sharp safety-reserve joke. [Movieclips: The Final Swim](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ll5qiWa6YDk) is a suitable source lead; verify playback and the released dialogue before quoting it.

- **Proposed family/concepts:** Accidents; Safe Exploration → Reversibility.
- **Meme intent:** `single`, on an authentic adult ocean-swim frame: **When the agent spends the rollback budget on the task**.
- **Caveat:** This intentionally changes the emotional lesson from determination to lack of a recovery margin. The film is not depicting an AI rollout, a literal rollback budget, or a safe-exploration experiment. The dark ocean imagery is also a weaker fit for the CMS's bright-still preference.
- **Recommendation:** Keep as a meme reserve; do not prioritize as a database entry merely because the line is famous.

## Directions to skip

- **Jerome's silver medal as Reward Hacking:** Losing despite strong genetic expectations does not show exploitation of a reward mechanism. It supports a prediction-versus-outcome discussion, but adds little beyond the stronger scenes above.
- **Vincent as a Sleeper Agent:** Hidden identity does not establish a trigger that activates harmful behavior.
- **Genetic selection as Biological Risks:** The existing concept concerns assistance with harmful biological agents or weapons, not genetics in general.
- **The entire caste system as one scenario:** Useful source-level context, but too broad to provide one exact scene and recognizable image.

## CMS preparation checklist

Follow [Notion CMS conventions](../notion-cms.md) when turning selected ideas into entries:

1. Check live Notion for an existing **Gattaca** source or scenario before creating duplicates. The local snapshot is only a baseline.
2. For a new Media Source, use **Gattaca**, movie type, a concise premise, and an authoritative trailer if found. Use the companion source note for the official Sony page/trailer lead. Upload exactly one representative source image; a poster is acceptable.
3. Use the short titles above for `Example`; leave `Episode` blank and `featured` unset. Keep taxonomy relations in the authored order.
4. Transfer each selected scene, analogy, and caveat as separate editorial fields. Preserve the distinction between plot evidence and our AI interpretation.
5. Review candidate clips for exact scene coverage, playback, and absence of commentary, reactions, or parody. A source lead is not a completed media check.
6. Select and inspect a clean authentic still, preferably at least 1200 px wide and roughly 16:9. Download it and upload the local file to Notion, with exactly one image in each page body. No images have been acquired or uploaded for this brainstorm.
7. Meme captions here are mutable concept-only semantic intent. No source image has been visually inspected and no finished meme has been rendered. Use the project's creator/composer workflow after frame selection; preserve faces, props, and readouts that make the scene recognizable.
8. Publish selected, completed Notion content with the documented sync workflow. Do not hand-edit generated snapshots.
