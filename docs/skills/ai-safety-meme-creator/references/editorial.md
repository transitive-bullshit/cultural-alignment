# Editorial branch

Use this branch while the concept, wording, source frame, or semantic mode remains open.

## Find the direction

Write a private brief with exactly one item in each field:

- recognition hinge: the smallest exact quote, name, number, prop, action, reaction, relationship, or plot mechanic that identifies the scene;
- AI bridge: one concrete AI or AI-safety concept;
- realization: why the visible scene literally demonstrates the bridge;
- must-preserve evidence: the faces, reactions, props, gestures, readouts, subtitles, or source-native marks that carry recognition.

Generate genuinely different directions by changing the hinge, comic ingredient, or semantic mode. Prefer the direction with the most recognition and payoff per word. Replace any direction that needs plot-summary prose, a second abstraction, or an explanation after the joke.

Strong editorial lenses include canonical material that already carries the joke, one precise technical relabel, a promise colliding with a visible outcome, character-owned dialogue, or a real state contrast. Technical language earns space only when it precisely renames what the viewer sees.

Aim for precise weirdness and the source's natural register. A specific blunt, profane, emotional, dry, or strange phrase can beat sanitized explanation. Avoid generic status boilerplate, dashboards, taxonomy labels, invented explanatory dialogue, and labels spread across the whole scene.

## Choose the semantic mode

- `single`: the image supplies enough setup for one forceful beat.
- `setup-payoff`: two concise beats form one reversal or collision.
- `dialogue`: the joke depends on distinct speakers and each beat has a real speaker anchor.
- `state-contrast`: two distinct authentic frames prove a change of state in reading order.
- `source-native`: code, a meter, a readout, or another depicted interface is itself the joke.

The mode describes meaning, not physical geometry. Leave placement, wrapping, sizing, and fallback selection to the composer.

## Write the beats

- Default to one or two semantic beats. Mutable source-native copy may use a third short row only when the depicted interface is the recognition hinge; never turn ordinary exposition into a status panel.
- Keep mutable copy to 24 words or fewer. Prefer deletion over smaller type, a taller panel, extra labels, or a more elaborate template.
- Put recognition before abstraction when possible.
- Name a referent when a pronoun would require hidden context.
- Preserve exact canonical wording only when supplied or verified. Mark knowingly altered source grammar as an intentional rewrite rather than a quote.
- Author semantic beats with intentional spelling, case, and punctuation. The composer applies any display-case transform; do not rewrite the stored beat to imitate rendered typography.
- Keep code characters and semantic indentation exact.
- Omit cosmetic terminal periods from generated meme fragments. Preserve punctuation that changes meaning or cadence, plus all punctuation in locked copy.
- End when the realization lands.

At the 480-pixel review preview, every complete caption must render at 18 pixels or larger. If exact locked copy cannot meet that floor, return a typed block. If copy is mutable, shorten or simplify it and compose again.

Select an authentic frame where the hinge is visible. Prefer another authentic frame over reconstructing the scene. A state contrast needs two genuinely different source images; never duplicate one image to simulate change.

Before composition, ask whether a fan can recognize the exact moment, whether one AI mapping is clear, whether the image proves the caption, and whether anything can be removed without weakening the realization.
